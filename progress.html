Begin working with the electronics and circuit boards, learn the manufacturing process, and continue developing our team and project website.

CompleteWeek 5September 25, 2026

Learning About Systems and Building Mock Payloads

This week, we learned more about system block diagrams, our Flight Data Recorder (FDR), and Arduino programming. We also started a new payload-building project that will challenge us to design and build a payload using foam board.

What We Accomplished

We completed a team-building activity where we practiced brainstorming and creating system block diagrams by designing our own vending machines. We learned more about the FDR and its components with Rick and continued working with Tera Term. For our Arduino lab, we created a new program that controlled multiple LEDs to make a traffic-light pattern.

Problems Encountered

We had to learn how different parts of the FDR, Tera Term, and Arduino work together. We also had to think about size, weight, and strength while planning our new payload.

What We Learned

We learned how system block diagrams can help us plan and understand how different parts of a system work together. We also learned more about the FDR, Tera Term, and Arduino programming. The payload project taught us that engineering designs have to follow specific requirements and limitations.

Next Steps

We will begin building our foamboard payload in our groups of two. The payload must stay under the required weight and be strong enough to hold as many golf balls as possible when dropped. We will also continue working with the FDR, Tera Term, and Arduino while preparing for the payload competition.

CompleteWeek 6October 2, 2026

Building Our Payload and Testing the FDR

This week we focused on two big things: finishing our foamboard payload housing and starting to test our Flight Data Recorder (FDR). We built and drop-tested the foamboard box, worked on our battery setup, and used Tera Term to communicate with the FDR for the first time. Rick Sparber helped us understand how the FDR operates.

What We Accomplished

Built our foamboard payload box and participated in the contest weigh-in and drop testing. Constructed a battery and learned how to measure its voltage. Tested our FDR using Tera Term to check its operation. Connected our RunCam camera to the FDR. Checked battery voltage readings and confirmed connections. Learned more about the FDR and its functions with help from Rick Sparber.

Problems Encountered

One challenge was learning how to use Tera Term and understanding how the FDR communicates with our equipment. We also needed to make sure our battery connections and voltage readings were correct.

What We Learned

We learned how Tera Term allows us to communicate with the FDR and check its operation. We also gained a better understanding of how our camera and battery connect to the FDR. Rick Sparber helped us understand more about the FDR and what it does.

Next Steps

Connect our sensors to the FDR, check their readings, and continue preparing our equipment for the payload.

CompleteWeek 7October 9, 2026

Connecting and Calibrating Our Sensors

This week, our team focused on connecting and testing the sensors that will be used in our payload. We worked with our temperature sensors, pressure sensor, and accelerometer. We also checked voltage readings and worked on equations to help us understand the measurements collected by our FDR.

What We Accomplished

Connected our temperature, pressure, and acceleration sensors. Checked voltage readings and collected measurements from each sensor. Worked on calibration equations to convert voltage readings into real measurements. Identified which FDR ports are used for each sensor. Continued improving our System Block Diagram based on feedback.

Problems Encountered

One challenge was making sure our measurements were correct and understanding what each equation represents. We also needed to make sure we were using the correct voltage readings for each sensor port.

What We Learned

We learned that sensors produce electrical signals (voltages) that need to be converted into useful measurements. Our calibration equations help us convert voltage readings into temperature, pressure, and acceleration values. We also learned why it is important to keep track of which sensor is connected to each FDR port.

Next Steps

Continue checking our calibration results, update our System Block Diagram, and prepare our equipment for the final payload housing.

CompleteWeek 8October 16, 2026

Payload Prototype and More Testing!

This week, our team focused on integrating the various components of our payload with the FDR. We worked on connecting and testing our sensors, camera, and GPS, while continuing to finalize calibration equations for our analog sensors. We also began developing a prototype of our payload housing, estimating its total weight and battery runtime, and working on our Arduino Lab #8 programming assignment.

What We Accomplished

Connected our sensors, camera, and GPS to the FDR and continued testing their functionality. Worked on finalizing calibration equations for our analog sensors. Began developing a prototype of our payload housing and estimating the total payload weight and battery runtime. Updated our System Block Diagram to include the sensor connections and corresponding FDR ports. Also reviewed the material for Arduino Lab #8 and began working on the menu-driven EEPROM storage program.

Problems Encountered

One challenge was making sure all components were properly connected and communicating with the FDR. We also needed to verify our weight and battery runtime estimates, which required extensive testing and troubleshooting.

What We Learned

We learned more about integrating multiple components into a single data retrieval system and the importance of accounting for payload weight and power consumption. We also gained a better understanding of how EEPROM memory can be used to store and retrieve data and how arrays can help organize information within a program.

Next Steps

Continue testing the FDR and verifying our sensor calibration equations. Refine the payload housing prototype, finalize weight and battery runtime estimates, update our team's webpage and System Block Diagram, and continue developing the Arduino Lab #8 program.

CompleteWeek 9October 23, 2026

Almost At The Finish Line

This week, our team focused on completing our payload and doing more extensive testing! We conducted preliminary tests under simulated conditions, reviewed the results, and considered any necessary modifications.

What We Accomplished

Continued assembling and refining our payload. Performed preliminary testing under simulated conditions and reviewed the results for potential improvements. Worked on confirming the payload's total weight and expected battery runtime.

Problems Encountered

One challenge was identifying issues during simulated testing and troubleshooting extensively to iron out any more issues. Multiple sensors broke and other parts needed to be re-soldered. This is what testing is about!

What We Learned

We learned the importance of testing our payload under simulated conditions before launch and using test results to identify areas for improvement. Testing reveals any faults with our payload that we still need to work on and shouldn't be taken lightly!

Next Steps

Finalize any remaining payload modifications, verify its total weight and expected battery runtime, and ensure all components are functioning properly.
