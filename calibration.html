Sensor Calibration

How we turn voltage readings from our sensors into measurements we can understand.

What Is Calibration?

A sensor gives the FDR an electrical reading. Calibration helps us convert that reading into a useful measurement, such as temperature, pressure, or acceleration. Our team checked voltages and took measurements to work out the equations below.

Our Five Equations

Inside Temperature — T1

T₁(v₀) = (v₀ − 0.457143) / 0.0108571

FDR port V0

Outside Temperature — T2

T₂(v₁) = (v₁ − 0.446471) / 0.0116286

FDR port V1

Air Pressure — P

P(v₄) = 3.745 × v₄ − 1.2645

FDR port V4

X-Axis Acceleration — Ax

Ax(v₅) = (v₅ − 1.73) / 0.14

FDR port V5

Y-Axis Acceleration — Ay

Ay(v₆) = (v₆ − 1.5625) / 0.1165

FDR port V6

In these equations, v means the voltage measured at the numbered FDR port. The output units should be confirmed against our team’s calibration notes.

Why This Matters

After the flight, we can use these equations to understand the numbers recorded by the FDR. We will also check that our readings make sense before launch.
